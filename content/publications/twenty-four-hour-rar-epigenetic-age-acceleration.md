Cross-sectional study of 207 middle-aged and older adults from the Baltimore Epidemiologic Catchment Area Study linking multidimensional 24-hour rest-activity rhythm features from wrist actigraphy with DNA methylation-based epigenetic age acceleration.

Stronger and more regular rhythms were associated with lower GrimAge and PhenoAge acceleration, whereas greater fragmentation and more variable activity timing were associated with higher acceleration. The findings support actigraphy-derived rest-activity rhythms as scalable behavioral markers of biological aging.
