Our article, [Twenty-Four-Hour Rest-Activity Rhythms and Epigenetic Age Acceleration in Middle-Aged and Older Adults](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2848752), has been published in JAMA Network Open.

Using 7-day wrist actigraphy and DNA methylation data from 207 adults in the Baltimore Epidemiologic Catchment Area Study, we found that stronger and more stable rest-activity rhythms were associated with lower GrimAge and PhenoAge acceleration, while greater rhythm fragmentation and more variable activity timing were associated with higher acceleration.
